I have been busy in the National Archives and the Oxfordshire History Centre researching the history of the Harwell site - from AERE Harwell to today!

My first post in a series will be about the 'Aicraft Incident' of the 23rd of April 1957.

---

By 1957 the Atomic Energy Research Establishment at Harwell was deep into nuclear energy research, with multiple reactors, such as GLEEP, BEPO, LIDO and DIDO operational for research. 

But just over 10 years earlier, the site was RAF Harwell and used as an airfield in World War II. Although, as shown in the image below, lots of construction had taken place on site there were the still the unmistakable views of runways from the air.

Also in place was a red flashing beacon on site. These were usually used for marking Air Navigation Obstructions 500ft above ground level. The Harwell site did not have anything like this, and there is no evidence of why it was originally put there. In fact, at the time, only 3 other beacons existed in the UK, two on mountains and one on a television mast in Wrotham in Kent.

The exact wording of the UK "Air Pilot" is as follows:
> ATOMIC RESEARCH STATIONS
>
>The  Atomic  Research  Station  at  Harwell  (51°34'40"N 
>01°18'30"W)  is  marked  from  sunset  to  sunrise  by  a  red  beacon 
>flashing  fifty  times  per  minute.  When  flying  near  the 
>Research  Station,  or  the  one  at  Sellafield  (54°25'10"N, 
>03°30'15"W)  which  is  not  marked  by  a  beacon,  pilots  are 
>particularly  requested  to  maintain  a  minimum  height  of 
>1,500  feet  above  ground  level  in  order  that  they  may  clear 
>the  areas  in  the  event  of  engine  failure  or  other  emergency 
>necessitating  an  immediate  landing. 

_RAC, Part IV, Chapter 18 of the U.K. "Air Pilot", Airspace Restrictions and Hazards._

On the evening of the 22nd of April, a United States Air Force Jet Trainer T-33A Shooting Star was flying over the area with low fuel and a failed radio. The pilot had no idea of this location but saw the flashing beacon (intended to keep planes away!) and the runways, and thought that this meant AERE Harwell was an aerodrome, and landed on the ex-runway. He was unaware of RAF Abingdon or Greenham Common nearby.
